Convert Your PDF Resume to an Editable Word Document

Most job applications are fine with a PDF resume. But when a posting or portal specifically asks for Word, here's how to convert without breaking the layout you spent hours getting right.

PDF or Word: which should you send?

Unless a job posting says otherwise, PDF is the safer default — it looks identical on every device and can't be accidentally edited by whoever opens it. Convert to Word only when there's a specific reason to.

Keep it as PDF when...

  • The posting doesn't specify a format
  • You're emailing it directly to a person
  • Your resume has custom design elements
  • You want to guarantee it looks the same everywhere

Convert to Word when...

  • The application portal explicitly requires .doc/.docx
  • A recruiter asks to edit it directly
  • You're filling an older-style ATS upload field
  • You need to quickly tweak content before resending

Why some applications ask for Word

Applicant tracking systems (ATS) scan resumes to pull out your name, work history, and skills automatically. Older or simpler ATS software sometimes struggles with PDFs — especially ones exported from design tools with unusual text layers — and reads a plain Word document more reliably. That's the main reason a posting will ask for .doc or .docx specifically instead of PDF.

Checking the result

Simple, single-column resumes usually convert cleanly. If yours uses a two-column layout, icon bullets, or a designed header, skim the converted file for anything that shifted before you submit it — see PDF to Word Without Losing Formatting for what to look for and how to fix small layout issues quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Should I send my resume as a PDF or Word document?

PDF by default, since it preserves your layout everywhere. Switch to Word only when a job posting or application portal specifically asks for a .doc or .docx file — usually because their applicant tracking system reads Word more reliably.

Why do some companies ask for a Word resume instead of PDF?

Some older or simpler applicant tracking systems (ATS) parse Word documents more reliably than PDFs, especially PDFs exported from design tools like Canva. Asking for Word reduces the chance the system misreads your name, dates, or skills.

Will converting my resume from PDF to Word mess up the formatting?

For resumes built from a simple single-column layout, formatting typically carries over well. Resumes with heavy multi-column layouts, icons, or graphic design elements are more likely to need small manual adjustments after conversion — check the result before submitting.
